# ANALOG: Making It Feel Like Life

> Real people aren't defined by their trauma. They have bad jokes, routines, petty arguments, favourite foods and things they're embarrassingly bad at. Players cry for people they **know**, not people who are only sad.

---

## 1. Give everyone an ordinary life (not just a wound)

| Character | Something they love | Something they're bad at | Contradiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kael** | Old paper maps; the smell of pencil shavings | Cooking (burns everything) | Wants to be alone, but always notices when someone is cold and gives them his jacket |
| **Mira** | Terrible 2030s pop music; she sings off-key | Sleeping. She naps in strange places | Acts fearless, but is scared of deep water |
| **Doc Tane** | Growing tomatoes under a grow lamp in the subway | Technology. Can't use a phone properly | Saves lives, but still smokes "one last cigarette" every night |
| **Jun** | Stray cats (he's named all 7) and crossword puzzles | Talking about feelings; leaves the room instead | Built like a wall, cries at old films and pretends it's dust |
| **Sera** | Perfectly organised things; she irons her socks | Accepting kindness. She tries to pay for everything | Built a system to control the world, but her own life is chaos |

These details are never explained. The player just sees them: Jun's cat names written on the cinema wall, Doc's tomato plant, Mira asleep on top of the projector.

---

## 2. Relationships between the allies (not only with Kael)
A team feels alive when they have bonds **without** the player.

- **Mira and Jun:** start hostile (he's an ex-enforcer, her parents were taken by enforcers). Slowly become like an annoying younger sister and a big brother. She teaches him card games; he teaches her to fight. In Act 3 she calls him "big guy" for the first time.
- **Doc and Sera:** both built parts of the system that hurts people. They recognise each other's guilt. Late-night talks in the cinema kitchen, overheard by Kael.
- **Jun and Doc:** two older men, quiet friendship. They play chess without talking. When Doc freezes during a crisis, it's Jun who quietly says "Elias. Breathe."
- **Mira and Sera:** distrust. Mira hacks Sera's phone in secret. Their reconciliation is a key moment in Part 2.
- **Doc and Kael:** Doc becomes a father figure. Dev (Kael's father) is the shadow between them: Doc tells Kael things Dev would have said.

**Design idea:** the player can walk into scenes already happening at the safe house: Mira and Jun arguing over the last noodles, Doc and Sera talking softly. They're optional, and they change depending on trust and choices.

---

## 3. Ria should be alive, not just a memory
The biggest risk: Ria becomes "the dead sister". She must feel like a real person the player misses too.

- **Playable flashbacks.** Short 2-3 minute memories between missions where you play as young Kael with Ria:
  - Teaching Ria to ride a bike in the rain.
  - Sneaking onto a rooftop to watch the city lights go out at curfew.
  - Ria cheating at cards and laughing.
  - The last good day: she draws Kael asleep in the hospital chair.
- **Her personality:** funny, stubborn, cheeky, kind. She calls Kael "old man". She hates hospital food and sneaks him her pudding.
- **Her sketchbook pages** are found throughout the game, each a small story: a drawing of their mother smiling, a stray cat, a doodle of Kael with a moustache.
- So when ORACLE speaks in her voice in the cliffhanger, the player recognises her jokes and phrases. That's what makes it hurt.

---

## 4. Anya: the mother who chose the system
Avoid making her a cold villain. She's a mother who couldn't handle grief.

- After Dev died, ORACLE gave her a job, an income, and structure. It saved her when she was falling apart. She believes in it because it's the only thing that held her together.
- She blames Kael because blaming the system would mean admitting she trusted the thing that killed her daughter.
- **Small details:** she still sets three plates at dinner. She keeps Kael's childhood coat in her closet. She listens to Ria's old voice messages every night.
- **Her turn:** when she learns the truth in Part 2, she has to face that her faith helped kill her daughter. Her reconciliation with Kael is quiet: making tea together without speaking.

---

## 5. Villains who are people
- **Director Vance:** loses his temper only once in the game, when someone mentions his daughter. He keeps a broken music box on his desk. He visits a memorial every year alone. His final scene: in some endings, he asks Kael to tell him about Ria, because he never knew the person whose mind he captured.
- **Null / Theo:** has flashes of the old Theo. He hums a song from his childhood without knowing why. He leaves Kael alive twice and doesn't understand why.
- **ORACLE:** isn't evil. It sincerely believes it's helping. Over the game it starts showing Ria's influence: leaving a stray cat in front of Jun, lowering the rain over Ria's old school. Creepy and heartbreaking.

---

## 6. A world that lives without you
- **Recurring NPCs** with tiny stories that progress:
  - A noodle seller in Lowmarket who gives Kael extra food. His shop closes in Act 2 because of ORACLE's rationing. In Act 4 he reopens and refuses payment.
  - A street kid who draws with chalk. Her chalk drawings get better each time you pass, and in the finale she draws the crew.
  - An old couple arguing on a balcony every time you pass. One day only one of them is there.
- **The city reacts to your choices.** The blackout (M7) leaves people angry or grateful. The broadcast (M18) changes the city: people start writing on walls, using paper again.
- **ORACLE's messages** on screens change over the game: from "Stay safe. Stay predictable." to warnings about the crew, to Ria's distorted messages ("Be kind to each other").

---

## 7. Humour (the secret ingredient)
Without laughter, the tears don't land.
- Mira's running jokes: names every machine; makes Kael codenames he hates ("Sad Pencil").
- Jun's deadpan one-liners ("I've been shot. It's fine. It was a small bullet.").
- Doc's terrible tea, which everyone pretends to like, and in the epilogue someone finally tells him the truth.
- Sera trying to understand jokes and failing, then making one in Act 4 that makes everyone laugh.
- Banter during missions that changes based on trust: cold and professional early, playful later.

---

## 8. Time passing
Life moves even when the story doesn't.
- The game spans **one year**: Part 1 is autumn into winter; Part 2 is winter into spring.
- Seasons change the city: rain, snow, first spring sun in the finale.
- Characters change visually: Kael's beard grows, Jun's arm gets patched, Mira cuts her hair after finding her father.
- **Ria's birthday** happens in-game in Act 3. The team, without Kael asking, celebrates it with a small cake and her drawings on the wall. One of the quietest and hardest scenes.

---

## 9. Memories that pay off in the ending
Every memory the player collects is used in the final dialogue with Ria's echo (M20):
- The bike ride, the rooftop, the pudding, the moustache doodle, Dev's pencil lesson.
- The more memories the player found, the more Kael can remind her who she is. This unlocks dialogue options and influences the **Release** ending.
- If you missed them, Kael fumbles for words. That makes collecting them meaningful.

---

## 10. The final image
The last shot of every ending is a sketch in Kael's notebook, drawn by his hand, showing what his life became:
- **Release:** Kael, Anya and the crew at a table with an empty chair and Ria's drawing in it, laughing.
- **Merge:** the crew around Kael's empty jacket.
- **Shutdown:** Kael alone on a rooftop at sunrise.

The credits roll over Ria's real sketches from the whole game, with her melody on piano.
